1Executive summary

Across the 2023–2025 landscape of LLM-agent benchmarks that test agents through games and interactive environments, no existing benchmark unifies the four properties a palace-intrigue social-decision benchmark would combine:

  1. (a) a persistent numeric relationship / dignity / status state,
  2. (b) hidden-information social dynamics (deception, secrets, asymmetric knowledge),
  3. (c) rigorous planning-quality metrics, over
  4. (d) a long horizon.

The prior art each covers a strict subset. Strategic-planning benchmarks have planning rigor but use abstract games with no persistent social state; social-intelligence suites have rich rubrics but short, dyadic horizons; generative-agent sandboxes have long-horizon emergent dynamics but are not goal-directed and score no planning quality. The intersection — long-horizon, goal-directed planning over an evolving relationship/dignity state under hidden information, packaged as a reusable benchmark — is essentially unoccupied. That is Red Dust's niche.

Closest prior art

CICERO (Meta's human-level Diplomacy agent) is the nearest neighbour because it genuinely fuses strong planning with deep social play — but it is a single fixed game with no persistent status ledger, and it is a bespoke agent, not a reusable benchmark. The boundary it does not cross is exactly the one Red Dust is built around.

3The landscape: four clusters

The relevant 2023–2025 work falls into four groups. The first three are the directly comparable “social” lineage; the fourth is the broad game-benchmark backdrop that frames the field.

Cluster A — Strategic planning + negotiation

Fuse formal planning with multi-agent social games. SPIN-Bench is the single closest prior art for Red Dust's priority (planning) track; CICERO proves long-horizon social play benefits from a dialogue model coupled to a separate planning engine; Werewolf Arena tests deception and persuasion in hidden-role play.

Cluster B — Social-intelligence rubrics (dyadic, short-horizon)

SOTOPIA contributes the most transferable social rubric (7 customizable dimensions incl. Relationship and Secret-keeping) and quantifies the LLM-as-judge validity ceiling; AgentSense adds bottom-up scenario generation from movie scripts and triangulated Self/Other/External scoring. The SOTOPIA-π / Sotopia-RL / S4 / AMPO successors advanced training and infrastructure but left the evaluation dyadic and per-episode.

Cluster C — Generative-agent sandboxes (long-horizon, emergent)

Generative Agents (Smallville) established the memory→reflection→planning loop and objective long-horizon behavioural metrics; AgentSociety is the closest precedent for explicit numeric relationship state (typed edges with 0–100 strength) at scale.

Cluster D — Broad game/agent benchmarks (non-social backdrop)

Rigorous outcome scoring over heterogeneous games, but single-agent or asocial — useful as method references and contrast points, not competitors. Detailed in §5.

7The gap Red Dust fills

Positioning statement

Red Dust is the first benchmark to evaluate long-horizon, goal-directed planning over an evolving numeric relationship/dignity/status state, under hidden-information social dynamics. SPIN-Bench has planning rigor but abstract games and no persistent social state; SOTOPIA / AgentSense have social rubrics but short dyadic horizons and no planning-quality metric; Generative Agents / AgentSociety have long-horizon emergent dynamics and even numeric relationship state, but are not goal-directed and score no planning quality; CICERO / Werewolf Arena have hidden-info deception and ToM, but in fixed-rule games rather than an open narrative world with evolving status.

Empirical support that a harder social-decision benchmark is warranted: on SOTOPIA-hard, GPT-4 reaches goal completion of only 4.85 vs human 6.15, and fails at strategic communication (it bids literally at its target instead of anchoring; 45.5 words/turn vs humans' 16.8). Frontier models measurably underperform humans on long-horizon social goal achievement.

8Design inspiration: a layered scoring model

The most directly reusable output for Red Dust is a composite evaluation that layers verified methodologies. Each layer is independently grounded in the prior art.

  1. Outcome layer (SPIN-Bench / CICERO) — goal achievement, status/resource controlled, and percentile vs a human or baseline distribution.
  2. Social-rubric layer (SOTOPIA) — the 7 customizable dimensions plus a bespoke Dignity / Status dimension; scored by LLM-judge with its known validity ceiling, and triangulated Self / Other / External (AgentSense) to reduce single-judge bias.
  3. Long-horizon behavioural layer (Generative Agents) — objective, automatable proxies: information diffusion, relationship-network density evolution, coordination/event success — computed over the dignity/relationship state.
  4. Planning-quality layer (SPIN-Bench) — depth-weighted N-Step Look-Ahead and top-k vs an expert policy; borrow normalization ideas from LMRL-Gym (50 = beat-the-data baseline), SmartPlay (gap-to-human) and BALROG (0–100 progression).
  5. Ablation layer (Generative Agents) — ablate memory / planning / theory-of-mind to causally attribute long-horizon social outcomes to specific capabilities.

Additional portable mechanisms worth stealing: SmartPlay's capability × game matrix (report which social skill is missing), Voyager's iterations-to-milestone (planning efficiency without a solver), Jericho's walkthrough/valid-action handicaps (tractable + comparable), Sotopia-RL's offline utterance-level credit assignment, and GAMA-Bench's dynamic parameterization (anti-leakage).

9Caveats & limitations

  • LLM-as-judge validity ceiling. SOTOPIA's own data: GPT-4 vs human Pearson r≈0.71 for Goal, but only ~0.45–0.62 for soft dimensions (Relationship, Believability). Auto-scored dignity/relationship metrics need human validation.
  • NPC fidelity risk. A critique of AgentSociety (arXiv:2507.19364) finds LLM agents show 20–300× lower behavioural heterogeneity than humans (“average persona”). Red Dust must show its NPC adversaries are diverse enough that scores reflect genuine skill, not exploitation of predictable NPCs.
  • Coverage. The verified core rests on the 25 claims that survived verification; the field moves fast and newer successors likely post-date this corpus.
  • Map interpretation. The positioning map's y-axis is a deliberate analytical choice (persistence-weighted), not a neutral measurement.
  • Attribution. Dramaturgical / ERG theory and script-based scenario sourcing belong to AgentSense, not SOTOPIA — do not misattribute in writing.

10Open questions

  1. No-solver planning optimality. SPIN-Bench's N-Step / top-k metrics assume an optimal solver or ground-truth plan. In an open social world there is none — how do you define plan optimality for intrigue?
  2. Measuring dignity reliably. How to make a numeric dignity/status state both mechanically consequential and reliably measurable, given the soft-dimension judge ceiling (r≈0.45–0.56)?
  3. Validating NPC difficulty. How to verify that LLM-driven NPC adversaries are behaviourally diverse and challenging enough to make measured performance meaningful?
